How it works. The match state is placed in a band of overs (1–6, 7–11, 12–15, 16–18, 19–20), wickets down (0–1, 2–3, 4–5, 6+) and scoring demand (the current run rate when batting first, the required rate when chasing). Each player's balls in that exact band count in full, and balls in a neighbouring band count at 35%. Value is runs gained (batters) or saved (bowlers) per 100 balls compared with an average player in the same spot, after adjusting for the opposition, venue and season. Wickets count at their run cost in that match state. Recent seasons count more, IPL balls count fully, and T20Is count at 50% against top sides and 25% against others. Where a player has only a few similar balls, his value is blended with his overall pressure form, so a lucky handful can't top the list. Bars show how much evidence sits behind each value. #4 marks a place in our overall top-10 pressure rankings. Win probability and stakes come from the same model used for the rankings.
